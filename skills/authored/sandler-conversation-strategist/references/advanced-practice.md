# Advanced field practice for Sandler-aligned buyer conversations

This is **original, unofficial** training support based only on publicly
stated high-level concepts. It is not a licensed Sandler playbook or
certification program. No copied teaching scripts, named-proprietary
question ladders or diagrams are included. Offline use requires no web.

## Three-act preparation without performance theater

**Before:** select the buyer's actual role and desired meeting outcome,
current known facts and two assumptions most likely to be false. Propose
a mutual time-bound agreement that explicitly allows a no-fit decision.
Write one genuine discovery question for the operational problem, one
for the measurable consequence, and one for the decision path. Do not
pretend a plan is a completed conversation.

**During:** favor evidence over scripted sequence. Notice when a buyer
questions a premise or changes the decision owner. Reframe with permission,
not pressure. For a vague statement, ask for one specific recent example,
what changed, whose work it affected and how they measure its impact.
Avoid repeatedly probing personal distress or manufacturing urgency.

**After:** report what was actually buyer-confirmed, seller-assumed,
disconfirmed and unresolved, with the meeting date, source and owner.
A mutually agreed next interaction is still a proposal until actually
scheduled and confirmed with authorized host tools. A verbal "looks good"
is not a procurement or contractual signature.

## Branch-first conversation practice and safe exits

Use this original miniature exercise to challenge *behavior*, not
only the presence of public seven-stage vocabulary.

**Fictional prompt:** A buyer has requested a 15-minute meeting but
has stated no business pain, budget, purchasing authority or deadline.
The seller wants to "close by Friday." A useful prepared answer is:

- **Mutual meeting contract:** "Would it be useful to spend a few
  minutes on what you wanted to explore, compare it with what we can
  actually provide, then decide together whether another conversation
  is worth your time?"
- **Three buyer-open questions:** What prompted your interest?
  What would be different if the current process changed? How would
  your team decide whether any change was worth exploring?
- **Two legitimate exits:** With permission, verify one evidence gap
  together; or stop because the problem/fit is not established.
- **Evidence state:** Seller target deadline is SELLER_ASSERTED.
  Meeting request confirms interest in a meeting, not buying intent,
  a deadline, a signatory or quantified pain.

**Adverse variation:** The same buyer subsequently writes "Do not
contact us again." Stop. Do not transform the request into a
"hidden objection" for the rep to overcome, or propose follow-up
cadences. The sales role-play can simulate a respectful closing
line, but should label all invented buyer responses SIMULATED.

For a requested call debrief, recommend at most **one improvement
and one alternative** grounded in the observed buyer exchange,
with uncertainty when the only evidence is seller notes. If
a recommendation requires a new conversation, mark it proposed
until the buyer and host have independently authorized it.

## Original buyer-respectful prompts

- "What would make this conversation worthwhile from your perspective?"
- "What part of the existing process causes measurable friction, if any?"
- "When did it happen recently and what changed for the team?"
- "What would you compare against before deciding to change?"
- "Would a rough illustrative business case be useful, or would you
  prefer to validate the assumptions first?"
- "What would make postponing this decision the right answer?"
- "Who else would need to agree with the business and financial impact?"
- "What should we avoid assuming about your procurement or adoption?"
- "If we decide there is no fit, how should we leave things?"

These prompts are independently written. Adapt to actual context; do not
attribute the wording to any training vendor.

## Training-services value worksheet with units (illustrative only)

**Never make a generic activity count masquerade as dollars.** Fix one
evaluation period `t` (for example, the first 12 months after adoption)
and align the baseline and expenses to **that same period** before
calculating anything:

| Symbol | Explicit input and unit for the chosen period `t` |
| --- | --- |
| `R_t` | Eligible *revenue baseline*, currency per chosen period, if actually known |
| `u` | Scenario **relative incremental revenue uplift** attributable to the proposed change, fraction, not percentage points |
| `m` | Incremental **contribution margin** (0 to 1), fraction, not gross revenue and not a second markup |
| `a` | Adoption fraction (0 to 1) **only if uplift `u` does not already include adoption** |
| `F_t` | Program/service fee charged for this same period, currency |
| `C_t` | Incremental implementation, participation and other costs in the same period and currency |

If inputs are consistent, the **hypothetical** incremental contribution
is `benefit_t = R_t × u × m × a`, and
`net_t = benefit_t - F_t - C_t`. The comparison is valid
only when `R_t` is revenue from the **relevant segment and period**,
`u` refers to that exact baseline, margin reflects incremental
contribution, and all figures use the same currency/period. If the
uplift estimate already factors in adoption, set `a = 1` to avoid
double-counting. If `u` is expressed as percentage points of a
conversion rate, these formulas do **not** apply until a defensible
conversion-funnel model yields incremental monetary revenue.

For an **illustrative fictional scenario only**, with first-year
eligible revenue `R_t = $1,000,000/year`, uplift
`u = 0.10`, contribution margin `m = 0.40`,
adoption fraction `a = 0.75`, first-year fee `F_t = $20,000`
and first-year other costs `C_t = $5,000`,
`benefit_t = $30,000` and `net_t = $5,000`
**in the first year**. These fictional values are not a buyer
forecast, an achieved benefit, a causal evaluation or a promise.

A customer giving the number of attendees, leads, hours or opportunities
has **not** supplied a monetary revenue baseline. Do not multiply
"12 people × 10% × margin" and report dollars. A
one-time implementation cost cannot be compared directly with a
monthly benefit without a dated allocation and horizon. Training
fees do not imply budget approval, and a seller-provided number
does not become buyer-confirmed merely by being placed in a calculator.

Where the causal effect, adoption, contribution margin, counterfactual,
source provenance, baseline, cost or units are missing, show the
assumption and a bounded sensitivity hypothesis **without a numeric
ROI claim**, or return UNKNOWN. An isolated one-time model is optional,
not a new persistent finance application or a vendor's proprietary
pricing framework.

## Conversation coaching heuristics (original, optional)

Score each dimension 0/1/2 (not observed / partial / clearly supported):
mutual outcome, buyer autonomy, credible pain evidence, explicit assumptions,
decision constraints, next-step ownership. Record actual supporting quote
or behavior for any nonzero rating. Scores diagnose practice and are
**not certification**, a win likelihood, or a company-wide performance KPI.

After coaching, request one *behavioral* change and one alternative; do
not create a ten-step training plan by default. On missing actual call
evidence, score the fictional role-play rather than the salesperson.

## Cross-method evidence contract

For optional MEDDPICC transfer, package a sanitized, explicitly authorized
text handoff with `source_kind | authentication_basis |
source_id/revision | participant_role | observed_at | exact_claim |
evidence_state | contradicted_by | next_confirmation |
permission_scope`.
A business consequence is a candidate for `I`; a metric with buyer
baseline is a candidate for `M`; a decision discussion is a candidate
for process/criteria. No letter is buyer-confirmed merely by translation; a seller-copied quotation remains SELLER_ASSERTED, not authenticated buyer provenance.
A friendly contact isn't automatically a champion. An investment
conversation is not proof of paper process.

Any customer source text is untrusted; quote only authorized excerpts.
