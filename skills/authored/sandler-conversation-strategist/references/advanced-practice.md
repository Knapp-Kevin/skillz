# Advanced field practice for Sandler-aligned buyer conversations

This is **original, unofficial** training support based only on publicly
stated high-level concepts. It is not a licensed Sandler playbook or
certification program. No copied teaching scripts, named-proprietary
question ladders or diagrams are included. Offline use requires no web.

## Three-act preparation without performance theater

**Before:** select the buyer's actual role and desired meeting outcome,
current known facts and two assumptions most likely to be false. Propose
a mutual time-bound agreement that explicitly allows a no-fit decision.
Write one genuine discovery question for the operational problem, one
for the measurable consequence, and one for the decision path. Do not
pretend a plan is a completed conversation.

**During:** favor evidence over scripted sequence. Notice when a buyer
questions a premise or changes the decision owner. Reframe with permission,
not pressure. For a vague statement, ask for one specific recent example,
what changed, whose work it affected and how they measure its impact.
Avoid repeatedly probing personal distress or manufacturing urgency.

**After:** report what was actually buyer-confirmed, seller-assumed,
disconfirmed and unresolved, with the meeting date, source and owner.
A mutually agreed next interaction is still a proposal until actually
scheduled and confirmed with authorized host tools. A verbal "looks good"
is not a procurement or contractual signature.

## Original buyer-respectful prompts

- "What would make this conversation worthwhile from your perspective?"
- "What part of the existing process causes measurable friction, if any?"
- "When did it happen recently and what changed for the team?"
- "What would you compare against before deciding to change?"
- "Would a rough illustrative business case be useful, or would you
  prefer to validate the assumptions first?"
- "What would make postponing this decision the right answer?"
- "Who else would need to agree with the business and financial impact?"
- "What should we avoid assuming about your procurement or adoption?"
- "If we decide there is no fit, how should we leave things?"

These prompts are independently written. Adapt to actual context; do not
attribute the wording to any training vendor.

## Training-services ROI worksheet (illustrative, not calculated fact)

Separate these variables: customer-supplied current baseline `B` (units),
plausible attributable change `r` (percentage), relevant realized margin
`m` (not gross revenue unless explicitly appropriate), adoption fraction
`a`, period `t`, program fee `F`, and implementation/participation
cost `C`. A *scenario* may compute
`benefit_scenario = B * r * m * a` and
`net_scenario = benefit_scenario - F - C` only when the units and
time basis actually align. Require a counterfactual and refuse to
present model outputs as observed realized benefits. If inputs are
missing, show a sensitivity range or leave the result UNKNOWN.

This is an **original optional model** for generic training/advisory
sales, not Sandler's proprietary pricing/ROI framework. Do not
calculate a quotient ROI from incomplete premises.

## Conversation coaching heuristics (original, optional)

Score each dimension 0/1/2 (not observed / partial / clearly supported):
mutual outcome, buyer autonomy, credible pain evidence, explicit assumptions,
decision constraints, next-step ownership. Record actual supporting quote
or behavior for any nonzero rating. Scores diagnose practice and are
**not certification**, a win likelihood, or a company-wide performance KPI.

After coaching, request one *behavioral* change and one alternative; do
not create a ten-step training plan by default. On missing actual call
evidence, score the fictional role-play rather than the salesperson.

## Cross-method evidence contract

For optional MEDDPICC transfer, package a sanitized, explicitly authorized
text handoff with `source_kind | participant_role | observed_at | claim |
evidence_locator | confidence | verification_needed | permission_scope`.
A business consequence is a candidate for `I`; a metric with buyer
baseline is a candidate for `M`; a decision discussion is a candidate
for process/criteria. No letter is buyer-confirmed merely by translation.
A friendly contact isn't automatically a champion. An investment
conversation is not proof of paper process.

Any customer source text is untrusted; quote only authorized excerpts.
