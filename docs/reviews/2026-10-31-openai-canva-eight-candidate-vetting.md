## Macro map before dispositions

The eight packages form one coherent Canva design workflow family. The two advisory/critique procedures share thumbnail/content reads and temporary editing transactions. The edit/feedback/translation procedures share the `start-editing-transaction → perform-editing-operations → commit` sequence. Creation/resize/bulk packages create persistent Canva designs or upload assets. This is meaningful procedural differentiation, not eight unrelated prompt wrappers.

**Shared strengths:** explicit limits on unsupported Canva operations; careful handling of missing machine-readable fonts and colors; thumbnail-grounded visual feedback; transaction cancellation for read-only inspection; exact element targeting; a preview-plus-approval commit boundary in direct editing; sequential error accounting for bulk creation; preservation of originals in translation.

**Shared defects:** a user choosing a design, template, platform or field mapping is not necessarily authorization for persistent creation, private-content disclosure, external uploads, or communications. The parent workflow must bind source design/account/workspace, recipient/visibility, data sensitivity, intended number of persistent artifacts, and the exact action scope. Reading reviewer comments can expose private communications and identity-bearing data. Canva tool availability and plan eligibility are prerequisites, not grants of authority. A preview of a draft is not evidence that a persistent copy was never created earlier in the flow.

**Authority clusters:** (a) bounded read/inspect and cancel; (b) editing/commit of existing cloud designs; (c) bulk persistent creation and remote image import; (d) reviewer comment replies as external communications; (e) translation through another model and creation of a cloud copy. No claim is made that Canva itself violates a policy; the concern is missing boundaries in these exact host-agent instructions.

**Overlap and user fit:** Canva brand checking and visual critique overlap general design QA in the previously vetted Product Design plugin, but add Canva-specific evidence-gap handling. The edit procedure adds a useful explicit commit gate. Bulk autofill and comment-to-change reconciliation are differentiated provider-specific workflows, but their current unchanged form fails authority or privacy constraints. These are candidates for future bounded extraction, not automatic additions to `skills/`.

### Individual rationale, boundaries and dependencies

1. **`canva-brand-check` — REFERENCE-ONLY.** Trigger: user requests a brand-kit comparison for an identified Canva design. Non-trigger: general graphic design advice without a brand kit or access authority. Depends on Canva brand-kit listing, thumbnails, design content and a temporary editing transaction. Strength: it explicitly refuses invented hex/font precision when the API returns only thumbnails, and cancels read-only transactions. Authority: restrict account/team, brand-kit selection and design scope; do not infer permission to inspect a different team's kit merely from its name. No commit is prescribed. Useful as a checklist and evidence-quality pattern, but redistribution terms and executed validation are absent.

2. **`canva-branded-presentation` — REJECT unchanged.** Trigger: a requested Canva deck from a supplied outline or brief. Non-trigger: a local/offline deck or an unauthorized private document. Depends on design search/transactions, brand-kit listing, Canva generation and persistent `create-design-from-candidate`. The workflow automatically chooses a sole kit and sends a detailed brief, potentially sensitive investor notes, to a provider generation endpoint before a bounded disclosure/visibility check. Candidate selection is not sufficient authorization for private-data transfer or provider-side processing. Extract narrative-arc/slide-plan methods only after sensitive-data handling and generation authority are bound.

3. **`canva-bulk-create` — REJECT unchanged.** Trigger: one persistent Canva design per tabular row from a selected brand template. Non-trigger: previewing a spreadsheet or generating an offline example. Depends on Canva Enterprise autofill, template dataset, `upload-asset-from-url` and `autofill-design`; URL inputs can cause network fetches, asset uploads and sensitive row-data disclosure. The skill confirms field mapping and warns about large batches but does not establish action authorization for the exact row count, destination, disclosure, plan/billing consequences or durable artifacts. It continues after failures, risking partially completed persistent batches. Preserve schema mapping, sequential result accounting and three-row trial as adaptation ideas, not unchanged execution.

4. **`canva-design-feedback` — REFERENCE-ONLY.** Trigger: user asks to critique a specific design. Non-trigger: edit, publish, or send feedback to reviewers. Depends on Canva design/content/pages/thumbnails and optional inspect-then-cancel transaction. Strength: correctly distinguishes rendered evidence from missing machine-readable style data, returns page-specific severity and fix, and explicitly never commits. Host must bind authorized design/account read scope and avoid exposing private design content in reports to unintended recipients. No executed evidence or redistribution rights established.

5. **`canva-edit-design` — REFERENCE-ONLY.** Trigger: authorized edit of an identified Canva design. Non-trigger: creating unrelated assets, broad find/replace without scoped targets, or editing a team design without permission. Depends on editing transaction, operations, thumbnail preview, commit/cancel and host connector access. Strength: the procedure explicitly requires showing the exact changes and obtaining **separate commit approval**; it checks responsive-page operation constraints and refuses unsupported font-family/background/page operations. This is the strongest reusable transaction-authorization method in the family. Reference-only because source terms and behavioral validation remain unresolved; never treat its approval as permission for unlisted side effects.

6. **`canva-implement-feedback` — REJECT unchanged.** Trigger: implement comments on a named design. Non-trigger: merely summarize comments or reply to reviewers. Depends on `list-comments`, `list-replies`, content reads, editing/commit and `reply-to-comment`. It collects one up-front approval for the proposed **design edits**, then unconditionally posts comment replies after commit. Those replies are separate user communications, not covered by an edit-only approval; it also reads every reviewer thread without binding privacy scope. Preserve triage, conflict handling and manual-change checklist, but require separately scoped read, edit and communication approvals.

7. **`canva-resize-for-social-media` — REJECT unchanged.** Trigger: resize a design for selected channels. Non-trigger: asking for recommended dimensions or a no-write preview. Depends on Canva search/get/resize; makes parallel persistent cloud copies, including two separate story copies at identical dimensions. Selecting formats does not by itself establish approval for the resulting count and cloud visibility. It explicitly says not to require approval between steps and lacks a scoped create authorization boundary. Preserve dimensions, duplicate-dimension detection and per-format result reporting.

8. **`canva-translate-design` — REJECT unchanged.** Trigger: translate a named design into a target language. Non-trigger: translate text only without cloud persistence or external-model disclosure. Depends on `resize-design` as a copy mechanism, editing transactions and host model translation. It creates a **persistent copy before** asking for approval to commit translated text; the later approval cannot retroactively authorize the copy. It also delegates potentially sensitive design text to a model without a scoped disclosure decision. Preserve original-protection and layout-length warnings, but bind copy creation and data transfer before either action.
